The third row is the point

Tab tells you where to put your fingers. Notation tells you what the note is. This writes both, and puts the names underneath — so the shape under your hand and the harmony you're playing over sit on the same page.

You never type a note name. Put 4 on the D string and Fis appears. Change the string and it changes with it. Dutch naming, or English if you prefer (F♯, C♯, B♭).

In there: quarters, eighths, a dotted quarter and a tie; an eighth rest; two ghost notes; a triplet; a slide into the second bar and a hammer-on out of it; chord names; and a repeat written once instead of the bars copied out.

Chords first, notes later

Some songs come apart structure first — you catch the progression on one pass, then go back and work out what the bass is doing underneath it. The whole shape goes in as one line of typing.

  1. Type the progression

    [Verse] Am | C | G G7 | Dm

  2. Get the bars

  3. Work out the notes

You don't have to work out the notes alone

While you fill a bar in, the editor shows the chord you're under — its notes, which degree each one is, and every place they fall on the neck. Click a dot and that note lands under the cursor. No counting frets to find the third.

It reads majors, minors, sevenths, sixths, sus and diminished chords, with roots spelled either way. A slash chord keeps its bass note, which on a bass is the part that matters.

A bar to a line, or per |. Several chords in one bar split it between them. [Name] starts a section. Typing a fret over a rest turns it into a note and leaves the chord exactly where you put it.

What it does

Write it

  • Set a length, type fret numbers, space to move on. Both hands stay on the keyboard.
  • Bar lines place themselves from the time signature — you never draw one.
  • Rests, dots, ties and triplets. Dead notes, hammer-ons, pull-offs, slides.
  • Repeat marks with a play count, and 1st / 2nd time bars.
  • Chord names above, lyrics below, section headings like Chorus and Bridge.
  • Click a chord to see its notes on a fretboard, then click one to place it.
  • Copy a phrase and paste it further on. Undo everything.

Read it

  • Octaves in Helmholtz, scientific, Yamaha or written pitch — or left off entirely.
  • Choose how many bars go on a line, and break lines and pages where you want them.
  • Bar numbers, so "from bar 17" means something at rehearsal.
  • A section that just repeats an earlier one can say so in a word.
  • Prints clean to PDF: the music and nothing else, ready for a stand.

Play it

  • Hear it back from any note, or select a passage and hear only that.
  • Follows your repeats and endings, so what you hear is what's written.
  • Loop four bars at 60% and work them up to speed.
  • The page turns a line early, so there's always something to read ahead into.
